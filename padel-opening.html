The finalists

Why padel, and not the other two

The problem, precisely

Padel balls are pressurised to 10.1–11.4 PSI under FIP regulation VERIFIED. From the moment the can is opened they lose 1–2 PSI per week VERIFIED. That gives the problem three hooks, not one:

  • Money. A can of three is €5–€8, and regular players budget €5–€15 a month on balls alone VERIFIED.
  • Performance. Dead balls come off the racket with less energy.
  • Strain. Players compensate by hitting harder every shot — the industry links this to "padel elbow" VERIFIED. Frame this as performance, never as a medical claim.

The customer

25–50, urban, plays twice a week with the same three friends, owns a €220 racket he researched for a fortnight. Active European players spend €600–€1,200 a year on the sport; US enthusiasts $1,000–$2,000 VERIFIED.

That matters more than it looks: you are not creating a buying habit, you are redirecting one that already exists. That is a much cheaper marketing job.

The kills

Four I killed that looked good

You asked me to challenge you rather than please you. These are the rejections worth understanding, because each one is a rule.

Aquarium water changers — a severe problem, killed anyway

The pain is real and weekly. But the incumbent is loved: Python "outclassed the competition by a significant margin, with nothing else coming close" VERIFIED.

The rule: never attack a category where the leader is beloved. You win against mediocrity, not against affection.

Dog enrichment — booming demand, killed on arithmetic

A $2.8bn market heading to $5.1bn, with inherently viral content VERIFIED. But retail is $5–15. At £0.67 a click and 1.5% conversion you need roughly £45 to acquire a customer.

The rule: demand cannot rescue a price point. This is a bundle add-on, never a hero.

Magnetic windshield covers — perfect timing, killed on physics

Complaints are well documented: magnets scratch paint, covers blow off in wind, sizing doesn't fit VERIFIED. But "magnets scratch paint" is inherent to the mechanism.

The rule: you cannot differentiate your way out of a physics problem.

LED dog collar — best evidence in the report, killed on logistics

The gap evidence is extraordinary: "Most light-up collars found online are cheaply made and flimsy. When testing other LED collars, none impressed reviewers — buckles were flimsy, collars were stiff and scratchy, non-standard USB cables were required, LEDs looked cheap, and would likely not last more than one winter" VERIFIED. An entire category where every product is bad.

It lost on lithium batteries: UN38.3 testing, air-freight restrictions, CE/UKCA marking, electronics warranty and returns. You said no complicated batteries unless necessary.

Keep this one on file. It is the right third product for the dog brand once you have cash flow and a customs broker.

The thing nobody mentions

Dropshipping just became structurally unprofitable

Changed the whole model

The US $800 de minimis exemption ended 29 August 2025 for all countries. The EU's €150 exemption ended 1 July 2026, replaced by a flat €3 duty per item until July 2028, with a further €2 handling fee expected by November 2026 — €5 per declaration line. VERIFIED

Every parcel now carries duty and a customs entry. Direct-from-China dropshipping to the US and EU is broken.

The model that works now is bulk import → local 3PL → domestic delivery. Pay duty once at wholesale value instead of on every retail parcel.

Two consequences you need to plan around:

  • Inventory becomes your largest budget line, not ads. Any plan putting 70% into advertising and 10% into stock was written for the pre-2025 world.
  • It favours you. This raises the floor for casual dropshippers and rewards exactly the real-brand model you said you wanted.

Creative

Fifteen angles to test

All built to look native to Reels and TikTok. Three hooks minimum per concept — the hook is 80% of performance, and the first two seconds decide everything.

01 · Demonstration ★ test first

"Your balls are dead. Here's the proof."

Two balls, same drop height, locked-off camera. New one hits the line. Week-old one doesn't come close.

02 · Pain

"How many dead balls are in your car right now?"

Open a boot, tip fifteen dull balls onto the ground. "£40 of balls. All dead."

03 · Cost maths

"Padel balls cost me £190 last year."

Receipts on a table, then a calculator. "Two cans a month at £8. This cost £59 once."

04 · Before / after

"Day 1 vs Day 30."

Split screen, gauge reading both days, same number. "That's the whole product."

05 · Frustration

"That moment the ball dies mid-rally."

POV court footage, limp bounce, shoulders drop. "Then you start swinging harder."

06 · Comparison

"I bought four pressurisers. Three leaked."

Four tubes, gauges checked one by one. "You shouldn't have to trust it."

07 · Problem awareness

"Nobody tells you this when you start padel."

Talking head, court behind. The 11 PSI explanation, plainly.

08 · Authority

"My coach made me buy this."

Real coach, real club, unscripted. "He said I was hitting too hard to compensate."

09 · Strain ⚠ careful framing

"Why your elbow hurts after padel."

Slow-motion heavy swing vs controlled. Never say "prevents injury" — say players compensate.

10 · Gift · run Nov–Dec

"For the padel obsessive who has three rackets."

Clean unboxing. "They don't need another racket."

11 · Ritual

"Ten seconds after every match."

Hands drop balls in, twist, three pumps, into the bag. "That's the whole routine."

12 · Lifestyle

No hook — trending audio only

Morning light, glass court, kit on the bench, a good rally. Text overlay: "Match ready."

13 · UGC testimonial

"I was sceptical."

Creator filming in their car after a session. "Three weeks. Same three balls."

14 · Objection handling

"'Just buy new balls.' Okay, let's talk."

Direct to camera, slightly confrontational. "£8 a can, twice a month, forever. I'll wait."

15 · Insider

"Things only padel players understand."

Fast cuts — the squeeze test before serving, the disappointed nod. "We all do it."

Kill any creative at 2× target CPA with 1,000+ impressions and no purchase. Never run one that opens with your logo.

Execution

The first thirty days

Days 1–3 · Validate before you spend

Meta Ad Library check. Google Trends by country. Trademark screen. Message 8–10 suppliers. Buy 2–3 competitor products — you cannot make the comparison creative without them in your hands.

Stop condition

If Ad Library shows 20+ brands actively running padel pressuriser ads with long-running creatives, my saturation assessment is wrong. Switch to the dog robe.

Days 4–7 · Samples and foundation

Order samples with express shipping. Register the company and domain. Shopify Basic and a clean theme — not a premium one yet. Start posting on TikTok and Instagram immediately; you need two weeks of history before ads. Email 20 UK clubs. Register for the 4 November convention.

Week 2 · Build and film

Samples arrive — start a filmed 30-day pressure test on day one. That footage is your single most valuable asset, and if you don't start it the day samples land you won't have it when you need it. Place a trial order of 100–200 units. Build the landing page. Film 6–8 creatives. Verify the purchase pixel actually fires.

Week 3 · Soft launch

Stock lands. Seed 10 coaches with free units. Gather 15–25 genuine reviews before ads. Start £30–50/day Meta, £20/day TikTok Spark Ads. Post organically five times a week.

Week 4 · Read the data

Kill losers, scale winners at +50% no more than every three days, run the diagnostic below, make the reorder call.

Write this on a wall

ROAS below 1.2× after £1,500 across 10+ creatives and two landing pages means a product problem. Stop and move to the backup. The most expensive mistake in this business is not a failed test — it is refusing to admit a test failed.

Diagnostics

Which problem do you actually have?

Re-read this weekly. Most people fix the wrong thing — they make new ads when the page is broken, or redesign the page when nobody is clicking.

Is CTR below 1%?

→ Creative problem

The hook isn't stopping the scroll. Nobody is reaching your page, so your page cannot be the issue. Fix: three new hooks on your best concept — change the first two seconds only. Don't touch the website.

CTR fine · Is add-to-cart below 3%?

→ Landing page problem

They arrive and leave. Message mismatch, slow load, weak above-the-fold, or missing trust signals. Fix: make the page headline match the ad hook word-for-word. Don't make more ads.

ATC fine · Is checkout completion below 30%?

→ Offer / checkout problem

They want it, but something at the final step stops them — usually shipping cost shock or too few payment methods. Fix: free-shipping threshold, add Apple Pay, PayPal and Klarna, show total cost earlier.

Checkout fine · Is ROAS still below 1.8×?

→ Margin problem

Everything converts, the maths just doesn't work. Fix: raise AOV — bundle harder, add the post-purchase upsell, consider raising price. Don't discount; it makes a margin problem worse.

Everything healthy · ROAS below 1.2× after £1,500

→ Product problem

People are interested but not interested enough at this price. The product is not the one. Move to the backup. This is not failure — it is the test working correctly and cheaply.
