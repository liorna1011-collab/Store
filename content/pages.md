### The short version

A new padel ball is a pressure vessel. Under International Padel Federation rules it holds between **10.1 and 11.4 PSI** more than the air around it. That pressure is what makes it come off your strings the way it should.

From the moment you break the seal on the can, it starts leaking — roughly **one to two PSI a week** at first. Not through a hole. Through the rubber itself. Gas molecules are small, rubber is porous, and pressure always moves from more to less.

Nothing stops this. You can only slow it down.

### What a soft ball actually does to your game

The obvious part: it bounces lower and feels dead off the wall.

The part most players miss: **you compensate without deciding to.** A ball with less stored energy comes off the racket slower, so you swing harder to get the same depth. Every shot. For an hour and a half.

Players and coaches connect that compensation to the forearm and elbow complaints that show up two or three months into playing regularly. The ball is not injuring you — the way you start hitting to make up for it is what adds load.

*(We are not making a medical claim, and neither should anyone selling you something. If your elbow hurts, see a physio, not a shop.)*

### The two things that actually help

**1. Stop opening cans you are not about to finish.**
Free, and most people do the opposite — three cans half-used in a bag, all dying at the same rate.

**2. Keep opened balls above their own pressure.**
This is what a pressuriser is. A sealed chamber held higher than the ball, so the pressure gradient stops pushing outward. Balls stay at playing pressure several times longer, and recently-soft ones come back up over a day or two.

### The problem with most pressurisers

They leak.

Not dramatically — slowly, through a seal that was never that good, or one that has aged. And because a sealed opaque tube gives you no way to check, you keep believing it works. One manufacturer in this category says publicly that many users of a competing model report it simply does not work. Another puts it bluntly: a pressuriser that leaks is worse than no pressuriser at all, because it gives you false confidence.

There is a second problem, quieter: **pressure rating.** A chamber that holds 14 PSI is above a padel ball (10–11) but barely above a tennis ball (12–14). It will maintain, but it will not restore. Plenty of cheap tubes sit right there.

### What we did about it

We put the gauge on the outside.

That is the entire design decision, and everything else follows from it. You can check the pressure any time you open your bag. If it has dropped, you know immediately instead of six months later. And because you can check it, we can guarantee it: more than 1 PSI lost in 30 days and we replace the tube free.

A promise you can verify is worth more than a promise you cannot.

### The 90-day pressure guarantee

This is the one that matters, so here is exactly how it works.

**The promise:** your P4 Pressure Tube will not lose more than **1 PSI over 30 days** while sealed.

**How to check:** pump it to pressure, note the gauge reading and the date, and leave it sealed. Photograph the gauge on day one and again on day 30.

**How to claim:** email those two photos to us. If the drop is more than 1 PSI, we send a replacement.

**You do not send the old one back.** Posting a faulty tube back and forth costs more than the tube. Keep it, or give it to someone who can live with a slow leak.

**Valid for 90 days from delivery**, one replacement per order.

### Why we can offer this

Because the gauge exists. A guarantee like this is impossible on a sealed tube you cannot see into — neither you nor we would ever be able to prove anything. Being able to check is what makes the promise real.

### What is not covered

Damage from dropping it, running it over, or opening it up. Normal cosmetic wear. And balls — we cannot guarantee balls, only the pressure the tube holds.
